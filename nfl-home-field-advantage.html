Where visitors go to lose

The toughest home fields in NFL history, conference by conference, weighing longevity, winning at home, noise, weather and altitude. Plus every current stadium.

NFC

Cold, noise and old-school ownership culture separate the top of the conference.

Green Bay Packers

Lambeau Field, since 1957

The oldest continuously used stadium in the league, decades of winning at home, brutal late-season cold and frozen turf, and a fan base that owns the team. No NFC venue matches its combination of longevity and hostility.

  1. PackersLambeau Field

    The Ice Bowl and generations of December games that warm-weather teams dread.

  2. SeahawksLumen Field

    The loudest NFC building of the modern era. The 12th Man set crowd-noise records and visiting offenses pile up false starts.

  3. SaintsCaesars Superdome

    A dome traps the noise. The Brees and Payton years made it a nightmare, especially in prime time.

  4. EaglesLincoln Financial Field, formerly Veterans Stadium

    The conference's most famously hostile crowd. The Vet's rock-hard turf was a hazard of its own.

  5. Bears and 49ersSoldier Field; Candlestick Park

    Chicago winters, and the 49ers' 1980s and '90s dynasty at Candlestick, earn a shared spot.

AFC

Here the edge comes from physics as much as fans: thin air, deafening noise and lake-effect snow.

Denver Broncos

Empower Field at Mile High, formerly Mile High Stadium

The only advantage in the league that is physical rather than emotional. At 5,280 feet, visiting players tire faster, especially in the fourth quarter, and Denver has posted one of the best home records in league history across both Mile High stadiums.

  1. BroncosEmpower Field at Mile High

    Altitude wears opponents down regardless of crowd size or era.

  2. ChiefsGEHA Field at Arrowhead Stadium

    Holds the Guinness record for loudest outdoor stadium, and the Mahomes era turned it into a near-fortress in January.

  3. PatriotsGillette Stadium, formerly Foxboro Stadium

    The Brady and Belichick run produced one of the most dominant home stretches ever, plus plenty of snow games.

  4. SteelersAcrisure Stadium, formerly Three Rivers Stadium

    Decades of consistent winning, a rabid crowd and notoriously tricky footing late in the season.

  5. BillsHighmark Stadium, Orchard Park

    Lake Erie wind and snow plus one of the league's most devoted fan bases. The new Highmark Stadium opens a fresh chapter.
